Games & Developers

From Demon's Souls to Black Myth: Wukong, the games that forged the genre and the studios that carried it forward.

Evolution of the Soulslike Genre

The Soulslike subgenre went from a mad experiment to one of gaming's most influential forces. It all began with Demon's Souls (2009), where FromSoftware laid a foundation of punishing gameplay and immersive storytelling that would change the face of action RPGs. Dark Souls (2011) refined that formula, and its interconnected world design and slow, deliberate combat became the genre's trademarks.

As the genre matured, each new release pushed its limits. Dark Souls II (2014) and Dark Souls III (2016) cemented the fundamental pillars of the experience, while Bloodborne (2015) proved the formula could carry over to new settings and combat systems. Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice (2019) went even further with its emphasis on rhythm and precision, showing that the Soulslike framework could accommodate a variety of playstyles.

Elden Ring (2022) made the most ambitious leap yet, translating the Soulslike experience into an open world without sacrificing the difficult encounters and environmental storytelling at the heart of the genre. Its extraordinary success brought these design principles to their widest audience to date. Through it all, the essential philosophy has remained unchanged: meaningful difficulty, rewarding exploration, and cryptic storytelling that respects the player's intelligence.

Games That Defined the Genre

FromSoftware Masterpieces

FromSoftware · 2011–2016

Dark Souls Trilogy

The trilogy that defined the genre set a template countless games would follow. Its interconnected world design made exploration feel like solving a three-dimensional puzzle, and its storytelling, fractured across item descriptions and environmental details, created an entirely new kind of player engagement in which making sense of the world became a community effort.

FromSoftware · 2015

Bloodborne

Bloodborne's Victorian Gothic setting and horror elements transformed the Soulslike formula. Its rally system, which let players regain health by counterattacking immediately after taking damage, encouraged a far more aggressive style of play than the careful, methodical approach of Souls. Its nightmarish design and oppressive atmosphere created an unforgettable experience that many consider FromSoftware's artistic peak.

FromSoftware · 2019

Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice

Sekiro broke from tradition with a fixed protagonist in place of character customization. Its posture system focused on breaking an enemy's stance rather than whittling down their health, creating rhythm-based combat that feels like elaborate duel choreography. A grappling hook and stealth mechanics expanded its tactical options, and a resurrection mechanic added strategic depth to death itself.

FromSoftware · 2022

Elden Ring

Elden Ring brought the Soulslike formula into an open world while keeping its core tenets intact. The freedom it offered felt like a natural extension of the series' exploration-driven design, while its Legacy Dungeons preserved the studio's signature level design: intricate, handcrafted spaces woven into a vast world.

The Genre Expands

Team Ninja · 2017–2020

Nioh Series

Team Ninja took the Soulslike formula to feudal Japan, where a stance-based combat system introduced new layers of complexity. Its Guardian Spirits and yokai abilities form one of the most technically deep action systems in the genre.

Round8 Studio & Neowiz · 2023

Lies of P

This grim reimagining of Pinocchio moved the formula into a Belle Époque setting with steampunk aesthetics. Its weapon customization system offered unprecedented build variety, while its fairy-tale narrative took a more direct approach than the genre's traditionally cryptic storytelling.

Ska Studios · 2016

Salt and Sanctuary

This 2D side-scrolling take on Soulslike principles showed how they could work from a different perspective, staying faithful to the stamina-based combat, punishing difficulty, and interconnected world design that defined the genre.

Game Science · 2024

Black Myth: Wukong

Inspired by Chinese mythology, the stunning Black Myth: Wukong fused Soulslike combat with vibrant visuals that break from the genre's usual gloom. Its fluid combat and transformation mechanics, rooted in Journey to the West, brought welcome cultural diversity to the genre.

Respawn Entertainment · 2019

Star Wars Jedi: Fallen Order

Respawn successfully brought Soulslike mechanics into the Star Wars universe, offering an accessible entry point for new players. Its commercial success proved the mainstream potential of these mechanics when thoughtfully adapted.
